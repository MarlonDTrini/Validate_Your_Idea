The problem

A good idea isn't enough.

Entrepreneurs often make decisions while critical assumptions are still untested. The purpose of validation is not to prove that an idea is good—it is to discover what must be true for the idea to work.

Is there a real market?

Identify demand, customer needs, market conditions and relevant trends.

Who will actually buy?

Challenge assumptions about customers, value and willingness to pay.

Can the business work?

Explore costs, operations, resources, partnerships and potential roadblocks.

What could make it fail?

Expose risks, objections and deal-breakers before they become expensive.

“The goal isn't to prove your idea is good. The goal is to find out what the evidence says.”
The methodology

A structured six-phase validation process.

Move from an early-stage idea toward a clearer, tested and defensible business concept.

01 — FEASIBILITY

High-Level Feasibility Analysis

Examine market demand, competition, technology readiness, legal and regulatory obligations, and trends.

02 — ASSUMPTIONS

Assumption Validation

Identify assumptions, prioritise risk, design low-cost validation tests and evolve the concept from evidence.

03 — IDEATION

Detailed Ideation

Model financial requirements, operations, resources, partnerships and potential roadblocks.

04 — PROTOTYPE

Conceptual Prototyping

Develop the concept, communicate the proposition, gather external feedback and iterate.

05 — CRITIQUE

Idea Critique & Defence

Identify objections, develop evidence-based responses and prepare to defend the concept with confidence.

06 — VIABILITY

Viability Criteria

Define requirements and deal-breakers, review the findings objectively and determine the next decision.
